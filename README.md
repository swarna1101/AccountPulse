# AccountPulse

**Know what’s changing. Know what to say.**

A short briefing tool for Customer Success Managers. Before a customer call, it turns public information about a company into something they can actually use.

## The problem

A Customer Success Manager looks after several enterprise accounts. Before a check-in, they often search the web themselves: news, the company site, leadership moves, product launches, hiring.

Most of what they find is noise. And even when something matters, it is rarely written as something they can say to the customer. A news feed does not answer the two questions that matter before the call:

- What is happening inside this customer’s organisation?
- What useful insight can I share with them today?

## What you get

Type a company name. AccountPulse reads recent public information and returns a short brief:

- **What changed.** A few meaningful shifts, not a pile of articles.
- **Why it matters.** How that change shows up in the customer relationship.
- **What to say.** One natural way to bring it up on the call.

Under that are the supporting signals, the broader pattern, and the public sources the brief came from.

## How to try it

Open the site. Type any company and choose **Try any company**.

To see a finished brief before you search, open a sample: Canva, Stripe, or HubSpot. Those are prepared examples, so you can read the product first.

Companies you search are kept in the sidebar on that browser, so you can come back to them.

## Assumptions

- Public information is enough for a useful first brief. This does not look at private customer data, emails, or call notes.
- The last few months matter more than old company history.
- A few strong signals are better than a long list of news.
- If there is not enough credible public information, the tool says so. It does not fill the gaps.
- The suggested line should sound like a well-informed colleague, not a sales pitch.
- There are no user accounts. A company you add stays in that browser, on that computer.

## How it is shaped

The product is one reading experience, built around the next conversation.

1. **Start.** The first screen states the job and asks for a company.
2. **Search.** A live brief is built from recent public web information.
3. **Read.** The top of the brief is the one thing worth saying today. Below that are the signals, the pattern across them, and the sources.
4. **Sample.** Three prepared accounts show the product before a live search.
5. **Return.** The AccountPulse name brings you back to the start. The sidebar keeps the accounts you have opened.
